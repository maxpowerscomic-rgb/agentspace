the problem

You ship constantly. You post never.

Building in public is a second job

Every tool asks you to stop working, switch context, and go write a post. That's exactly the tax you won't pay — so your last "build in public" update was six weeks ago.

So the log writes itself

wiwo watches your commits and Claude Code sessions and builds the log automatically. Sharing costs about ninety seconds a day, all of it optional. Nothing required, everything editable.

how it works

Work. wiwo logs. Share in one press.

1

Point it at a repo

wiwo watches .git and your Claude Code session — read-only. New commits get logged the moment they land, never mid-build.

2

Your day fills in

A timestamped daily log with auto-written summaries and before/after screenshots. Drop an optional one-liner where a change deserves a story.

3

Compile & share

One button turns the log into a thread, organized by project, reshaped for X / LinkedIn / Bluesky / Mastodon / Threads. Copy it, or post natively.

what's inside

A dashboard, a diary, and a megaphone.

▦

Glanceable dashboard

One card per project: latest chat context, build status, and a prompt box that drives your live Claude Code session — streamed back token-by-token.

◷

Auto daily log

Commits + session transcripts become a chronological log with before/after images. It updates live as you commit.

🧵

One-press threads

Organized by project, metadata auto-written, every line editable, reshaped to each platform's format.

🚀

Author or post natively

Toggle between "just write it, I'll post" and native posting to your connected accounts — with the permalink returned.

🔥

Streaks & digests

A consecutive-days streak and a weekly rollup by project, so momentum is visible.

🔒

Local & private

Runs on your machine. Your code, keys, and tokens never leave it — tokens are encrypted at rest.

yours, on your machine

Nothing leaves your laptop

wiwo is local-first and bring-your-own-key. There's no wiwo server holding your source, your API keys, or your social tokens. It only ever reads your repos — it never writes to them, and it never interrupts your agent mid-build.
